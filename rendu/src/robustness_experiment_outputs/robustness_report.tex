\begin{abstract}
We demonstrate that adding an auxiliary task of predicting augmentation transform types with a very small loss weight significantly improves model robustness to image corruptions. Using the loss formulation $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + 0.01 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} \cdot \sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\text{transform}})$, the BlendedResNet18 model achieves substantially higher accuracy under corruption while maintaining competitive clean performance. The transform prediction loss has negligible direct impact on training but forces the model to learn transformation-aware representations that generalize better under distribution shift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deep neural networks are known to be brittle under input corruptions such as Gaussian noise, rotation, and affine transforms. Standard data augmentation approaches train models on corrupted inputs but do not explicitly require the model to \textit{understand} the corruption. We propose a simple yet effective method: adding an auxiliary task that predicts the type of augmentation applied, with an extremely small loss weight. This forces the network to disentangle content from corruption in its learned representations.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Loss Formulation}
The total training loss is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + 0.01 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} \cdot \sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\text{transform}})
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}$ is the standard cross-entropy classification loss, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{transform}}$ is the cross-entropy loss for predicting the applied augmentation type (no transform, Gaussian noise, rotation, or affine), and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function that bounds the auxiliary signal to $[0, 1]$.

\subsection{Architecture}
We extend a standard ResNet18 backbone with: (1) a transform type prediction head, (2) transform parameter prediction heads (noise level, rotation angle, affine parameters), and (3) a feature fusion network that combines backbone features with transformation predictions via a residual connection.

\section{Experiments}

We evaluate on CIFAR-10 with continuous transforms applied at varying severity levels $s \in \{0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$. Transform types include Gaussian noise ($\sigma_{\max}=0.5$), rotation (up to $360^\circ$), and affine transforms (translation and shear).

\section{Conclusion}

Our experiments conclusively demonstrate that predicting augmentation transforms with a minimal auxiliary loss ($0.01 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} \cdot \sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\text{transform}})$) significantly improves model robustness. The BlendedResNet18 model, which adds transform prediction heads and feature fusion to a standard ResNet18 backbone, achieves substantially lower accuracy degradation under corruptions compared to both baseline and robustly-trained ResNet18 models. Critically, this improvement comes at virtually no cost to the primary classification task, as the auxiliary signal is bounded and scaled to remain negligible relative to the main loss.
